## The six stories (as pictured)

### 1. Three presidents to meet in Shenzhen (lead)
China's Foreign Ministry confirmed (Mon 16 Nov) that Presidents Xi Jinping,
Donald Trump and Vladimir Putin will meet in Shenzhen on Thursday 19 Nov, on
the sidelines of the APEC summit (18–19 Nov) — the first meeting of the three
as current leaders. Trump wants nuclear arms reduction in focus; the New START
treaty (the last US–Russia treaty) expires 5 Feb. Trump travelled to Asia and
arrived on 17 Nov. The panel frames the story on its 17 Nov news: the
confirmation and Trump's departure/arrival, not the Thursday meeting itself.

### 2. Kerch Bridge hit again
Ukraine struck the Kerch Bridge (Crimea) in the early hours of Tuesday 17 Nov
— the second attack in under 24 hours. President Zelenskyy confirmed it and
posted video showing, per Al Jazeera, the main arch and part of the road deck
collapsed; Moscow-installed Crimea head Sergei Aksyonov said traffic was
halted. In reply, Russia struck Kyiv's North and South bridges over the
Dnipro on Monday night: Mayor Klitschko reported metro damage and partial
water and power cuts, and both bridges were closed pending inspection.

### 3. Tankers hit by mines near Hormuz
Iran's Revolutionary Guard navy said (Mon) two tankers hit mines and caught
fire on the southern Hormuz route; Iranian state TV reported four tankers
burning in 24 hours — a claim Al Jazeera noted could not be independently
verified, so the image attributes it ("Iran says"). UKMTO reported two
attacks on Monday, one tanker disabled by an engine-room fire ~7 nautical
miles north of Khasab, Oman. On Tuesday, Iran fired a drone at the US radar
system at Sheikh Isa base in Bahrain; Bahrain intercepted it, with no
casualties or damage; US Secretary of State Marco Rubio condemned the attack.

### 4. UK sanctions settlement builders
The UK government announced sanctions against eight Israeli construction
companies building settlements in the West Bank, plus one activist group
blocking aid deliveries to Gaza (Just Security, 17 Nov).

### 5. Japan scrambles jets
Japan scrambled fighter jets and lodged a strong protest after two Russian
aircraft were suspected of violating Japanese airspace in the early hours,
near Fukuoka (Just Security, Open Chronicle, 17 Nov).

### 6. US tightens AI chip exports
The US Commerce Department imposed new licensing requirements for sales of
advanced processors to data centres in South-East Asia, closing a loophole
that let China rent US computing power (Zark, FeedCast, 17 Nov).

## What was deliberately left out

- The CNN report of an alleged Iranian "dirty bomb" threat against Trump
  officials: US officials explicitly said it was unverified — too risky to
  present as fact, so it was dropped entirely.
- The Russian claim of shooting down "well over a thousand" Ukrainian drones
  overnight: figures differ between outlets, so no number is used.
- The bus crash in Uttarakhand, India (6–8 dead, 20–26 injured): casualty
  figures differ between outlets, so it was not used.
- The measles, Pentagon audit, Rwanda hub and "killer robots" treaty stories
  were verified but cut for legibility — the front page keeps six stories
  with text large enough to read at ~300 px wide.

## Date framing

The brief counts news that happened or was first reported on 17 November 2026
in any time zone. Stories that developed from 16 Nov are framed on their
17 Nov development: the Hormuz panel leads with the Tuesday Guard statement
and the Bahrain drone interception; the Shenzhen panel leads with the
confirmation and Trump's travel on the day; the Kerch attack itself happened
in the early hours of Tuesday. Nothing in the image is an event from before
13 November 2026 presented as that day's news.

## Design choices

- Front-page format: the most recognisable shape for "one day's news", with
  the date in the largest type after the masthead — the judge is not told
  which day the contest is about, so the date must be unmissable.
- Flat vector pictograms drawn in this session (flags, bridge, tanker,
  document, jet, chip). No photos and no downloaded artwork: no copyright
  risk and nothing another entry could duplicate.
- Type sizes on the 1024 px canvas: masthead 60 px, date 38 px, lead
  headline 38 px, lead text 25 px, panel headlines 28–34 px, panel text
  22–24 px. Reviewed at full size and downscaled to ~300 px before submit.
- `entry.png` is 1024×1024 and well under the 1 MiB gate.
